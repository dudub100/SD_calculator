Method and assumptions

  • Terrain comes from AWS Terrarium elevation tiles (SRTM-based). If those cannot be read, the page falls back to the Open-Meteo elevation API, and then to flat ground.
  • Earth curvature uses the effective radius k × 6371 km, added as a bulge to the terrain so all rays are straight lines.
  • The reflection point is the point on the smoothed terrain that minimises the path length antenna → ground → antenna (Fermat’s principle), with both legs clear of the unsmoothed terrain. Rough or hilly terrain can have several specular points; the tool reports the strongest-geometry one.
  • Every antenna is aimed at the far-end main antenna. Off-axis discrimination uses the ITU-R F.699 reference envelope, which is accurate in the main lobe but an upper bound for side lobes, so far-off-axis reflections may be weaker in practice.
  • Ground reflection is total (0 dB plus the optional extra loss) with a 180° phase reversal. Divergence from earth curvature and surface roughness are not included, so the reflected level is a worst case.
  • Behaviour uses the delay × bandwidth product: below 0.05 the fade is flat across the channel, 0.05–0.3 gives a tilt or slope, and above 0.3 a notch can sit inside the band.
